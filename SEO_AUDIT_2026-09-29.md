## Deeper follow-up: ranking vs. indexing (same-day re-check)

A second pass re-verified every item above directly (live HTTP checks against `bcvotewatch.ca`, not just the repo) and added live Google queries to separate "is the site broken" from "is the site outranked":

- `www.bcvotewatch.ca` → 301 to `https://bcvotewatch.ca/`; `http://` → 301 to `https://`; `.html` URLs → 308 to the extensionless canonical; `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, and `assets/og-image.png` all resolve. No `noindex` anywhere in the 110 HTML files. Meta descriptions are unique per page. Homepage is 7 KB HTML / 7 KB CSS — no performance issue. Confirms the technical-checks section above is still accurate and nothing regressed since it was written.
- Live `site:bcvotewatch.ca` returns indexed pages (homepage, candidates page, several riding pages) — the site is not de-indexed or blocked.
- Live queries for two of the site's own core target terms, `BC election 2026 polls` and `BC election 2026 date how to vote`, return **no bcvotewatch.ca result** in the visible results. Both are dominated by Wikipedia, Elections BC (the official government site), and national newsrooms (Global News, CBC/Yahoo/MSN syndication, City of Vancouver).

This changes the diagnosis. The Sep 22→27 impression drop is not a crawl, index, or markup bug — that path is confirmed clean. It is better explained by two compounding, non-code causes:

1. **Search demand itself fell.** Query volume for "BC election" terms spikes at a news event (the Sep 22 election call) and decays afterward; fewer impressions can happen at an unchanged rank simply because fewer people are typing the query that day.
2. **Domain age/authority, not on-page quality, gates head-term ranking.** A domain that is about a week old cannot out-rank Wikipedia and a provincial government site on head terms like "BC election 2026" through on-page fixes — those incumbents win on accumulated backlinks and trust signals, which markup changes don't create. The reported average position of 6.8 is consistent with this: it is almost certainly pulled up by low-competition long-tail queries (individual riding names, "[riding] 2024 results", direct/branded "bcvotewatch" searches) rather than by head terms, where the site currently has no visible placement at all.

### Revised, realistic target

"Every BC-election-related query lands on page 1" is not achievable through code changes alone, and no further on-page fix is indicated — the technical layer is already at its ceiling for a site this size. Two things remain genuinely actionable:

1. **Compete where the competition is thin.** The 93 riding pages, the by-election page, and specific long-tail questions ("who won Oak Bay-Gordon Head in 2024", "BC advance voting dates") face far less competition than head terms, and the site is already well built for them (unique title/H1/description, FAQ + Breadcrumb schema, sourced data). This is where real page-1 placements are attainable in weeks, not months, and is the metric to track instead of head-term rank.
2. **Build the off-page signals a code fix can't substitute for:** verify/submit in Bing Webmaster Tools separately from Google; seek backlinks from BC-focused communities and civic directories (anchor text pointing at specific riding/poll pages compounds #1); consider Google News/Publisher Center only if the site adds original reporting beyond aggregation; keep the daily update cadence, since freshness is one of the few ranking levers a new site can win on for a live news topic.
